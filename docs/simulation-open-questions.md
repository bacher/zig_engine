# Questions before implementing the simulation

This is the open decision list from the 2026-11-05 discussion. It accompanies the [design principles](../DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md), [game vision](game-vision.md), and [simulation notes](simulation-notes.md). It records questions, not proposed answers or a selected architecture.

Resolve the core questions before committing to the corresponding simulation implementation. Focused experiments may supply evidence for those answers. The [warehouse specification](../specs/features/warehouses.md) settles first-version storage contracts and records its implementation checkpoints; broader logistics questions remain tracked below. Feature-specific questions only need answers when that feature enters the implementation scope.

Keep the IDs stable when discussing these items. When resolved, mark the item complete and link the decision in the relevant specification or design document. A scoped deferral should identify the excluded behavior and when the question must be revisited. This list does not require deciding every long-term feature before the first simulation prototype.

## Agreed constraints to preserve

These decisions are already recorded and are not open questions:

- Optimize for an automated world, initially targeting 100,000 concurrent work and delivery tasks on a 4–8-core gaming PC with 32 GB RAM, ideally 16 GB.
- Throughput takes priority primarily in world simulation and the world-data service supporting it. Client threads may favor low latency where it preserves overall system performance and simulation throughput; client demand must not overload the world-data service.
- Tasks start from their prerequisites; persistent individual NPCs, worker assignment, approach journeys, and return journeys are unnecessary. NPC models and movement separation are client-side presentation.
- Consume required resources in full at task start, with consistent accounting. Delivery cargo remains cargo. First-version deliveries have fixed simulation-time duration and no route revalidation after departure.
- New work normally starts at processing/acceptance time. A simple dependency on a late-produced or late-delivered good alone, apart from food, uses an earlier start derived from its predecessor's scheduled completion when this is cheap to establish. Consume resources at actual acceptance; food need not have existed at the assigned earlier start. Complex resource dependencies and starts after an attempt failed for missing food use the default with no compensation. See the [late-processing decision](simulation-notes.md#late-processing-and-dependent-work).
- Compensation is bounded by the actual delay, 5 simulation seconds, and half the new operation's duration. Retain assigned and actual start times so newly accepted work is visualized from its beginning, with compensation-induced acceleration at most 2x. Compensation cannot cause instant completion or chains of overdue new work. Late client updates remain a separate presentation question.
- Stopping rejects new work, completes accepted obligations, and leaves the object intact. Reactivation and dismantling are separate actions; dismantling waits for obligations to finish.
- Simulation edits win concurrent conflicts with player edits. Eligible condition checks may use recent mixed observations only after individual discussion; resource accounting remains the baseline and memory safety remains required. Warehouse Q20 opens evaluation of specific measured accounting exceptions without selecting one.
- Support activity throughout the world and startup without a full-world scan. Brief delays retain normal speed; sustained overload slows a common simulation clock across the world while preserving accepted schedules and costs. Recovery returns to 1x without accelerated clock catch-up or time debt; pending work remains intact. Slowdown must be visible to players. See the [overload decision](simulation-notes.md#coordinated-slowdown-and-recovery).
- Capacity shortages defer new acceptance without resource consumption or task progress, preserving later reconsideration. Accepted obligations have processing preference without indefinitely starving new eligible work or active regions. Coalesced notifications must preserve resource changes, completions, and compensation timing; client demand is bounded and observation does not accelerate production. See the [work-budget decision](simulation-notes.md#bounded-work-and-fairness).
- Simulation runs while the world is running and not paused. Closed worlds and stopped servers earn no offline progress; loading resumes saved simulation time and task progress. A running dedicated server may continue without connected players. See the [world-time decision](simulation-notes.md#world-time-and-no-offline-progress).
- Normal simulation time advances at 1:1 real time with millisecond task-time resolution, not mandatory millisecond ticks. Preserve causality and resource consistency without requiring a global order or deterministic replay of independent events. Single-player pause and explicit owner/admin multiplayer pause stop the world; an individual multiplayer menu does not. Whole-process suspension adds no time or catch-up debt and preserves scheduled events; late processing follows the resolved **SIM-05** rules.

- Global overload feedback uses measured, averaged simulation-clock speed separately from FPS and an unobtrusive warning for sustained overload, with stable recovery and no repeated notifications during one episode. Aggregate measurements must remain inexpensive. Per-object explanations of delayed automatic starts and object-panel design are outside the current scope. See the [feedback decision](simulation-notes.md#global-overload-feedback).

- Each mutable fact has a clear authoritative source: world-data service owns base terrain/chunks, loading, and terrain publication; simulation owns task state, obligations, work/dependency/routing indexes, and the selected active construction targets; clients own presentation. Exclusive mutation grants and client publication may be batched. Warehouse state belongs to persistent simulation objects; world-data keeps terrain and derived spatial claims, and accepted deliveries own their cargo. Warehouse Q17 retains player inventory in world-data service; Q18 accepts coherent live commit and shared capture/WAL without per-item disk synchronization; other storage contracts, ownership units, threads, and synchronization mechanisms remain open. See the [ownership decision](simulation-notes.md#state-ownership-and-publication).

- Preliminary planning creates no accepted obligation. First-version acceptance and actual start are one coherent transition for payment, cargo ownership, task creation, and obligations; failed starts leave no partial effects. Completion accounts for its result, status, and fulfilled obligations coherently. Retries must not duplicate effects. See the [task-transition decision](simulation-notes.md#consistent-task-start-and-completion); coordination and persistence mechanisms remain open.

## Time, scheduling, and overload

- [x] **SIM-04 — Meaning of simulation time.** [Resolved](simulation-notes.md#world-time-and-no-offline-progress): normal 1:1 time, millisecond task-time resolution without millisecond ticks, causal/resource ordering without a required global total order or deterministic replay, explicit single-player and owner/admin multiplayer pause, and no offline or whole-process-suspension catch-up. A running unpaused server may continue without players; saved and scheduled work retains its progress and deadlines. Ordinary processing delays, dependent-task timestamps, and coordinated slowdown are defined by **SIM-05**. In-game day length is a separate balance choice.
- [x] **SIM-05 — Delayed processing and causality.** [Resolved](simulation-notes.md#late-processing-and-dependent-work): start at processing/acceptance by default; use earlier timing for a cheaply established dependency on a late-produced or late-delivered good alone, apart from food. Food is checked and consistently consumed at acceptance without historical availability analysis; complex resource dependencies use the default. Compensation is at most the actual delay, 5 simulation seconds, and half the operation's duration; preserve assigned and actual start times, and never instantly complete newly accepted work through compensation. After an attempt fails for missing food, a later start receives no compensation. Brief delays retain normal clock speed; sustained overload uses a common slower clock while preserving accepted schedules and costs. Recovery returns to 1x without accelerated catch-up, retaining pending work. See the [overload rules](simulation-notes.md#coordinated-slowdown-and-recovery). Numeric production-rate tolerances are deferred to prototype acceptance under **SIM-03**, thresholds to **SIM-07**, and work budgets/implementation to **SIM-06** and **SIM-08**; resolve these at their corresponding prototype/design stages.
- [ ] **SIM-06 — Bounded work and fairness (partially resolved).** [Decided](simulation-notes.md#bounded-work-and-fairness): defer new acceptance without payment or task progress, preserving reconsideration; protect accepted obligations and prefer processing them without starving new eligible work or active regions; combine recheck notifications only while preserving resource accounting, completions, and compensation timing; bound client demand without making production depend on observation. Equal CPU time per chunk is not required. Still open: concrete limits for pending events, routing requests and deferred work; bounded representation and retry of deferred admission; budget distribution between accepted/new work and regions; and client-request/subscription budgets. How do these budgets support the agreed common-clock slowdown and recovery policy? Design and measure these with **SIM-03**, **SIM-08**, and **SIM-12**.
- [ ] **SIM-07 — Overload feedback (partially resolved).** [Decided](simulation-notes.md#global-overload-feedback): show measured, averaged simulation-clock advancement relative to real time, separately from FPS, with an unobtrusive warning for sustained overload. Avoid transient warnings, repeated notifications within one episode, and unstable recovery transitions; use inexpensive aggregate measurements. Object panels and per-object explanations of delayed automatic starts are outside the current scope; future missing-resource displays belong to later UI design. Still open: numeric thresholds and observation windows for slowdown, warning, and recovery; the speed averaging window; and treatment of sustained event-processing lag at 100% clock speed. Select these during overload-control design and validate them with the prototype. Concrete layout and measurement implementation remain unselected.

## State ownership and consistency

- [ ] **SIM-08 — Execution and ownership model (partially resolved).** [Decided](simulation-notes.md#state-ownership-and-publication): world-data service owns base terrain/chunk data, loading, and terrain publication; simulation owns task state, obligations, work/dependency/routing indexes, and the selected active construction targets; clients own presentation. Each mutable fact has a clear authoritative source. Permit exclusive mutation for batches and batched client publication while preserving authoritative accounting. [Warehouse Q16](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q16--simulation-owned-warehouse-state-versus-pallet-owned-metadata) selects persistent simulation objects as warehouse authority, with aggregate incoming reservations and active capacity in that domain, world-data-owned terrain/derived spatial claims, and delivery custody for cargo in transit. Q17 retains world-data-owned player inventory; Q18 accepts the coherent live-commit/shared-capture baseline, with concrete access mechanics still open; other storage contracts and concrete record placement remain open. Compare a simple execution baseline with parallel processing of independent regions under exclusive write access. Still open: ownership units, thread topology, cross-boundary coordination, safe readers and data lifetime during modification/publication/unloading, transfer or revocation of exclusive access including player approach, and the clock/scheduling mechanism for shared slowdown. Which evidence selects among the [architecture candidates](simulation-notes.md#architecture-ideas-to-revisit-later)? Chunk ownership, central orchestration, and message-only worker communication remain unselected. [Warehouse Q22](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q22--initial-execution-of-simulation-owned-warehouses) selects first-iteration warehouse execution on the current service worker with separate state domains, bounded scheduling, and mixed-load validation; independent and partitioned simulation executors remain alternatives for scaling. The [construction ownership spec](../specs/features/construction-state-ownership.md) selects actual simulation-owned storage/mutation/persistence of nonempty blueprint targets, followed by integration into world-data chunks. Whole-box ownership, world-data-owned storage with batched technical access, and queued operations remain documented alternatives. Marker encoding (cell tag, bitset, or sparse discovery), composed physical reads, cross-site access, and coherent multi-chunk handback remain open; frequent construction, ordinary reads, memory, and completion bursts are required comparison workloads.
- [ ] **SIM-09 — Starting and completing work consistently (partially resolved).** [Decided](simulation-notes.md#consistent-task-start-and-completion): planning is preliminary with no obligation; acceptance, payment, cargo ownership where applicable, task creation, and actual start are one logical transition with no accepted-but-not-started state. Failed starts leave no partial spending or transfer. Completion applies results, completed status, and fulfilled obligations coherently; retries must not duplicate effects. Compensated timing does not move actual payment into the past. Still open: coordination across components/chunks, retry identity and recognition, and handling partial implementation failures. Warehouse Q23 selects live-session external-command deduplication and explicit resynchronization after session changes, without a durable per-click result history. Window/acknowledgment encoding remains open; simulation tasks retain their independent persistent retry/recovery obligations. Design these with **SIM-08** and **SIM-13**; no universal transaction system or per-operation disk write is required. Destination-capacity semantics depend on **WH-01**, obligation scope on **SIM-15**, and exceptional gameplay outcomes on **SIM-16**. [Warehouse Q20](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q20--measured-exceptions-to-resource-accounting) reopens narrow accounting exceptions for measured comparison; none is adopted. Compare coherent batching before attributing coordination costs to conservation itself.
- [ ] **SIM-10 — Simulation priority over player edits.** What defines concurrent conflict and the scope and duration of the simulation's guarantee? What happens to a conflicting player action: rejection, deferral, or another explicit outcome? How does the client reconcile its optimistic view while the simulation avoids rollback caused by losing to a player? How do stop requests participate in this ordering?
- [ ] **SIM-11 — Consistency policy per check.** Which checks in the first implementation require coherent state, and which explicitly permit mixed observations? For each relaxed check, what deviations are acceptable, are its limits measured in real or simulation time, and what happens when observation age exceeds the limit before result use? How are important derived states, such as room validity, invalidated or refreshed so they cannot remain wrong indefinitely?

## Events, persistence, and task lifecycle

- [ ] **SIM-12 — Discovering and waking work.** Which changes can make each action eligible, and how are the affected tasks found without scanning the world or all waiting work? How are missed, repeated, or combined notifications handled? What wakes work after a failed prerequisite becomes satisfied, without continuous polling of unchanged conditions?
- [ ] **SIM-13 — Saving and recovering active simulation.** What task state and active-emitter index must be persisted, and how are they kept coherent with world edits, resource accounting, and the existing [persistence system](persistence.md)? How are active work and stopped objects restored without a full-world scan, duplicate outputs, or repeat payment? What save frequency and crash-recovery policy preserve coherent start/completion outcomes, and how much recent progress, if any, may be lost? Warehouse Q18 accepts coherent live commit and a common capture cut in the existing shared WAL, without per-item disk synchronization; recent unsaved progress may be lost coherently. Capture integration, costs, and numeric loss bounds remain open. How are older saves without simulation state handled?
- [ ] **SIM-14 — Data availability beyond loaded regions.** How does distant work access the world data it needs when no client subscribes to those chunks? What data must stay resident, what may be loaded on demand or reconstructed, and what happens under memory pressure while tasks retain references to it?
- [ ] **SIM-15 — Boundary of accepted obligations.** What exactly must finish before an object is fully stopped? Does completion of an already accepted production job include a later output-delivery task, or can the object stop with output still present? What happens if it is enabled again before outstanding work finishes? Warehouse-specific inventory and dismantling rules belong to **WH-04**.
- [ ] **SIM-16 — Cancellation and exceptional failure.** Is explicit cancellation of accepted or started work supported in the first version, or only stopping future work? For supported exceptional failures, what happens to already consumed resources, cargo, task status, and outstanding obligations? How does an object avoid remaining permanently stuck while stopping? These answers must preserve the no-route-revalidation rule for active deliveries.

## Routing and client presentation

- [ ] **SIM-17 — Planning a new delivery.** Is routing mixed road/off-road travel or primarily road-based? What route quality is sufficient, how are time and cost calculated across terrain and the wrapped world, and what limits apply to route search? What itinerary or other progress data must be retained for completion, persistence, and visualization? Active journeys are not revalidated; only new planning consumes updated connectivity.
- [ ] **SIM-18 — Food and perishable cargo.** What happens when food being transported as cargo spoils in transit, and what expiration information travels with it? How are spoilage events scheduled efficiently? Food already consumed as payment is outside this question. Stored-food behavior and inventory effects must align with **WH-05**.
- [ ] **SIM-19 — Observing active work.** [Decided](simulation-notes.md#late-processing-and-dependent-work): retain assigned and actual start times; visualize newly accepted compensated work from its beginning, including pickup, with compensation-induced acceleration at most 2x. What other authoritative task and progress information must clients receive to visualize selected areas without scanning all tasks? How should late notifications, including those received after completion, be presented while preserving the intended beginning and speed limits? How are subscription changes, joining clients, and save/load distinguished from a newly accepted compensated task without changing the simulation? What timing freedom does purely visual approach, departure, terrain adaptation, or model reuse have while staying consistent with fixed authoritative task times?

## Warehouse contracts and logistics follow-ups

These questions are tracked in the [warehouse specification](../specs/features/warehouses.md): Q1 selects count-only stock with deterministic repacking and player displacement on overlap; Q3 selects aggregate destination capacity reservation at departure, protection against unreserved puts, and coherent reserved handover; Q4 selects explicit stop/enable and stopped, empty, obligation-free unregister/material change. Q2 fixes the 4A demand target, 6A new-admission ceiling, preservation of stock/promises within 6N, and active-before-disabled packing for A in 0..N. First-version warehouse behavior is settled; full demand allocation and perishability remain separate work. The unchecked items below track broader logistics and implementation decisions, not unresolved first-version storage rules. The warehouse reservation decision applies to warehouse destinations; the workstation output-slot example does not establish a universal policy for every buffer or production output.

- [ ] **WH-01 — Capacity at start and arrival (partially resolved).** [Decided](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q3--destination-capacity-and-overflow-wh-01): reserve aggregate warehouse capacity at accepted departure without assigning slots or relying on future consumption. For N-pallet storage with A active columns, present C plus promised incoming I plus new cargo q must fit within 6A; the physical invariant remains C+I<=6N. Unreserved puts cannot consume promised space; handover coherently replaces its own reservation with stored stock. Start/handover retries and save/load preserve cargo, stock, and promises. Q4 now preserves accepted deliveries through stopping and forbids unregister/material change until they finish and stock is empty. [Q2](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q2--what-does-active-capacity-restrict) resolves active-column reductions: apply A in 0..N immediately, target 4A, retain earlier promises within 6N, and fill active columns before disabled ones so disabled stock drains first. Re-enabling columns needs no unregister. Exceptional cancellation/failure remains under SIM-16. Concrete ownership/coordination remains under SIM-08/SIM-09; no arrival-only capacity policy or waiting-for-space delivery queue is selected.
- [x] **WH-02 — Inventory and destination representation (gameplay resolved).** [Decided](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q1--one-count-versus-priority-draining): one record holds a single material's count, relative rectangular bounds, and active-column configuration. [Q16](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q16--simulation-owned-warehouse-state-versus-pallet-owned-metadata) now places that record, incoming promises, and lifecycle in a persistent simulation object addressed by WarehouseId; derive packed-goods layout/collision without authoritative item positions or column heights. Repacking, otherwise valid manual puts, and accepted delivery arrivals displace an intersecting player into known free space; player overlap does not block handover or change the deterministic layout. [Q26](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q26--reserved-halo-displacement-fallback) selects the reserved base-level halo after ordinary recovery fails, preferring known full-body destinations and acquiring bounded missing data without delaying simulation; no halo-floor requirement is added. [Registration](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q6--discovering-the-rectangle-and-choosing-the-layout-origin) recognizes the entire connected rectangular pallet component and selects a central layout-origin coordinate independently of the clicked member; future in-place expansion preserves that coordinate, but is deferred under [Q11](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q11--expansion). This iteration requires stopped, empty, obligation-free unregister followed by rebuilding and a fresh registration/origin selection to change bounds. [Q5](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q5--exact-center-and-ordering) retains the lower-coordinate central layout origin, uses separate hash bits to choose a central first column and preferred direction, and defines a deterministic order that completes inner layers before outer ones and leaves remaining corners last. A 4×4 base always fills its inner 2×2 to height four before the perimeter. [Q10](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q10--allowed-materials-and-pallet-selection) confirms eleven initial cargo materials including pallets and excludes water pending a separate liquid-mechanics design. Unregistered pallets can be recovered as carried items in three seconds and reinstalled in one second; registered members require unregister first. Pallet cargo uses one stock unit and the same roughly 100 ms transfers as other cargo. Observer edits are instant one-unit stock changes using the selected material; mismatches share the player-mode error and preserve stock/selection, while warehouse admission guards still apply. Key 9 selects pallets; a general observer material menu is deferred. [Q12](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q12--missing-warehouse-state-discovery-and-streaming-ownership) selects a WarehouseId/registration/bounds reference in every chunk intersecting the full reserved envelope, with at most eight entries and no stock copies. Local claims remain discoverable without resident object data; nearby queries load required object snapshots and treat possible cargo cells as unknown until resolved, while distant visual omission remains allowed. Ordinary stock changes do not rewrite these references. [Q15](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q15--courier-loading-and-unloading-points-wh-02) selects pickup/drop-off from any traversable side-adjacent base-level halo cell, excluding diagonal corners, with at most twenty candidates independent of stock/active capacity. Registration/manual use does not require a route; new automatic deliveries do. Later endpoint or route loss does not change accepted journeys or handover, and no exclusive dock or congestion queue is introduced. Q24 selects independent deduplicated object subscriptions with complete snapshots and functional client retention; concrete index recovery, encoding, and budgets remain implementation review. [Q25](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q25--incremental-publication-follow-up) defers comparison of increment/decrement or hybrid publication, with full initialization/resynchronization retained. No pallet chunk is required to own or load stock. Q17 retains world-data-owned player inventory, with the coherent cross-owner transfer/capture protocol under Q18; route search and endpoint selection belong to the separate routing design, and supplier allocation belongs to WH-03.
- [ ] **WH-03 — Demand and allocation (partially resolved).** [Warehouse Q2](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q2--what-does-active-capacity-restrict) selects the capacity-based deficit `max(0, 4A - C - I)`; Stop/Enable still gates new automatic work. How are requests created, aggregated, matched to suppliers, and prioritized? Which push/pull rules, supply areas, food-payment sources, and replenishment controls apply? How is scarce stock shared between competing requests without overcommitting it?
- [x] **WH-04 — Stopping and dismantling warehouses.** [Resolved](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q4--empty-is-not-obligation-free-wh-04): Stop closes new automatic jobs involving the warehouse, including inbound/outbound deliveries, while accepted work finishes with its cargo and reservations intact. Pending unaccepted requests cannot start while stopped. Manual take remains available, and completion leaves the warehouse intact and inactive. Enable explicitly reopens admission without resetting existing stock or obligations. Unregister/material change require stopped, empty storage with all accepted obligations finished; material change retains registration and stopped status. Neither stopping nor draining queues automatic deletion. Save/load preserves stopped intent and accepted work. General production follow-up obligations and exceptional cancellation remain SIM-15/SIM-16, not additional warehouse lifecycle choices.
- [ ] **WH-05 — Perishability and inventory changes.** How do spoilage, storage conditions, partial quantities, and food-crate disappearance or movement change inventory and wake dependent work? What is the handoff to delivery cargo and its expiration rules in **SIM-18**?

## Feature decisions when those features enter scope

These are relevant to the game vision but do not all block the initial simulation. **SIM-01** determines which need answers now. Record concrete rules in their feature specifications rather than deciding them indirectly in core infrastructure.

- [ ] **FEATURE-01 — Production and local services.** What operations, recipes, durations, output slots, and service-coverage rules are initially supported? What happens when coverage or facility validity changes during already paid work? Are food-dispenser rate limits admission limits, and what does each limit mean over time?
- [ ] **FEATURE-02 — Construction and extraction.** How are construction sites and deposits divided into tasks, and what makes the next task eligible? Which support or blueprint checks can use mixed observations, with exactly what accepted deviations? How are finite deposits accounted for consistently when extraction tasks compete? The construction ownership spec selects nonempty-target storage in simulation and handback, while task/cargo division and applied-site cancellation remain separate decisions. [Q21](../specs/features/warehouses.md#q21--optional-soil-transformations) opens removal/simplification of compaction, including placement-time side effects; remaining grass behavior and timers need an explicit policy.
- [ ] **FEATURE-03 — Further operating resources and transport.** Which clothing, tools, vehicles, fuels, or power mechanics are in scope? What is consumed, what is reusable, and what becomes unavailable to other tasks during use? Any consumable cost must respect the agreed payment-at-start rule; vehicle state must not silently reintroduce a persistent-worker requirement.
- [ ] **FEATURE-04 — Local progression and hazards.** How do housing, service quality, climate, or hazards affect task prerequisites, costs, and outcomes under the local-workforce model? Which proposed interactions require their own decisions about already accepted work and fixed delivery schedules? NPC model presence cannot decide these outcomes.
